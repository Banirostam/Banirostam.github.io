My research brings together computational approaches to artificial intelligence with philosophical investigations into cognition, intelligence, and the mind. It addresses both the development of intelligent systems and the conceptual assumptions underlying their design, evaluation, and interpretation.

## Research Areas

### Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning

My technical research focuses on machine learning, intelligent systems, and computational methods for analyzing complex data. Areas of interest include predictive modeling, explainable artificial intelligence, computer vision, and model evaluation.

### Data Analytics and Intelligent Decision Support

I investigate how machine learning, mathematical modeling, and optimization can support decision-making under practical constraints and uncertainty, particularly in data-intensive applications.

### Philosophy of Artificial Intelligence and Cognitive Science

My interdisciplinary research examines cognition, representation, meaning, agency, and the explanatory foundations of human and artificial intelligence. A central concern is how theories of mind inform the interpretation of contemporary AI systems.

### Artificial Consciousness

I examine conceptual and methodological challenges in assessing claims about artificial consciousness, including the distinction between functional intelligence and phenomenal experience and the evidential limits of consciousness indicators.

## Research Programs

### Mind Concepts — Ongoing

*Mind Concepts: Key Ideas and Connections in Mind, Cognition & AI*

An interdisciplinary research and educational initiative examining foundational concepts in philosophy of mind, cognitive science, and artificial intelligence.

The project investigates how concepts such as cognition, intelligence, perception, knowledge, representation, meaning, and consciousness are defined and related across different theoretical traditions.

**Resources:**

### Rethinking Mind — Completed

A research initiative tracing the historical development of theories of mind and cognition, from classical philosophical approaches to contemporary artificial intelligence.

The project examines how different intellectual traditions have shaped our understanding of intelligence, cognition, and mental phenomena.

Its outputs provide a historical and conceptual foundation for subsequent research on artificial intelligence and theories of mind.
